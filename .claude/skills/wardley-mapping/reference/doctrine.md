# Doctrine — Simon Wardley's universal principles

**Doctrine** = principles that are _universally_ useful regardless of context (unlike gameplay, which
is map-specific). Wardley lists ~40 principles in **six categories** and suggests adopting them in
**four phases** (each builds on the last). Use them as a checklist when reviewing a map or an org.

> Source: Simon Wardley, _Wardley Maps_ (Doctrine chapter), CC BY-SA. Exact wording/placement evolves
> over editions; treat this as a faithful working reference, not a verbatim copy.

## Adoption phases (the order to apply them)

- **Phase I — Stop self-harm.** The basics that stop an organisation hurting itself: _know your
  users, focus on user needs, use a common language (maps), be transparent, challenge assumptions,
  remove bias & duplication, focus on high situational awareness, use a systematic mechanism of
  learning, use appropriate methods/tools, think small (know the details)._
- **Phase II — Becoming more context aware.** Tune to the landscape: _use multiple/appropriate
  methods per evolution, think aptitude & attitude (PST), design for constant evolution, be the
  owner, manage inertia, manage failure, think small (small teams), distribute power & decision
  making, provide purpose-mastery-autonomy, think FIRE._
- **Phase III — Better for less.** Operational excellence: _effectiveness over efficiency, optimise
  flow, set exceptional standards, use appropriate standards, manage inertia (again), seek the best,
  listen to your ecosystems, exploit the landscape._
- **Phase IV — Continually evolving.** Leadership for a moving world: _there is no core (everything
  is transient), strategy is iterative not linear, commit to the cycle, a bias towards the new, be
  humble, embrace uncertainty._

## The principles, by category

### Communication

- **Use a common language.** A shared map removes translation errors between functions.
- **Be transparent.** Share maps openly so they can be challenged and improved.
- **Challenge assumptions.** Everyone has a duty to question, regardless of rank — constructively.
- **Know your users.** Customers, shareholders, regulators, staff — and their differing needs.

### Development

- **Focus on user needs.** Anchor everything on real needs, not internal/competitor obsession.
- **Use appropriate methods.** Agile/experiment in genesis, lean in product, six-sigma in commodity.
  _(The single most common map finding: one method applied everywhere.)_
- **Think FIRE** — Fast, Inexpensive, Restrained, Elegant — especially for the novel.
- **Be pragmatic.** Don't over-engineer the uncertain or under-engineer the industrialised.
- **Remove bias and duplication.** Aggregate maps reveal the same thing built many times.
- **Design for constant evolution.** Assume every component will move right; build for the shift.

### Operation

- **Think small (know the details).** You can't manage what you don't understand at component level.
- **Use appropriate tools.** Mapping + financial models + the right delivery tooling per stage.
- **Manage inertia.** Name resistance (sunk cost, contracts, ego, business model) and plan the move.
- **Manage failure.** Expect and contain it (more in genesis, far less in commodity).
- **Effectiveness over efficiency.** Doing the right thing beats doing the wrong thing cheaply.
- **Optimise flow.** Find and remove bottlenecks/blockers across the value chain.
- **Set exceptional standards.** Raise the bar; reuse good standards where they fit.

### Learning

- **Focus on high situational awareness.** Understand the landscape before deciding — the whole point.
- **Use a systematic mechanism of learning.** A bias toward data; learn patterns that repeat.
- **Listen to your ecosystems.** Treat partners/users as sensing engines for what's working.
- **A bias towards the new.** Continuously look for the next thing commoditisation enables.

### Leading

- **Move fast.** "An imperfect plan executed today beats a perfect plan executed tomorrow."
- **Commit to the cycle / strategy is iterative, not linear.** Map → act → re-map; never a one-off.
- **There is no core.** Everything is transient; no component is sacred forever.
- **Provide purpose, mastery & autonomy.** Direction + room to act.
- **Be the owner.** Take responsibility for the whole landscape.
- **Be humble; seek the best.** Listen, be selfless, hire/borrow the best, embrace uncertainty.

### Structure

- **Think small (small teams).** Cell-based, "two-pizza" autonomous teams around components.
- **Think aptitude and attitude.** Staff for skill **and** the right mindset → Pioneers / Settlers /
  Town Planners matched to each component's evolution stage.
- **Distribute power and decision making.** Push decisions to where the situational awareness is.
- **Design for constant evolution.** Build the org (and the "theft" mechanism) to keep moving.

## Using doctrine in a review

Walk the checklist and turn gaps into notes:

- No user/need anchor, or fuzzy users → **red** ("No clear user need anchored").
- One delivery method across all stages → **amber/red** ("Agile on a commodity — use utility/ops").
- Differentiator being outsourced, or commodity being hand-built → **red**.
- Visible inertia on a valuable component → **amber** ("Inertia: legacy contract").
- Strong situational awareness, clean value chain → **green** ("Clear, well-anchored chain").
- An evolution/co-evolution opportunity → **purple** ("Commoditise via utility → frees R&D").
